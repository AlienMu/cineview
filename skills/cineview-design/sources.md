# Drawing a page out of the product

A page that looks like its product usually took its colours, motion and motifs from that product.
These questions help find them. Ask the ones the material leaves open. The one-line examples come
from different fields to show the kind of answer, not the answer.

- **How does the product move, or move things?** That motion can become the page's motion.
  A camera opens like a shutter (a wipe from the centre). Coffee is poured (a rise from below, a
  fill). An accounting app settles numbers (a count that lands and holds). A fitness tracker
  pulses (a slow loop).
- **What is it made of, or what does it feel like?** That suggests texture, edges and palette.
  Paper and ink. Brushed metal. Glass and light. Linen. A screen at night.
- **What is its pace?** Calm and slow, steady, or quick and bright. This sets durations, easing
  and how much moves at once (`ranges.md`).
- **Where and how is the page read?** A commute on a phone, a desk at work, an invitation that
  arrives in a chat app, a screen in a shop. This sets the device, the density and the page mode.
- **What shapes and signs belong to its world?** One of them can return across the page as a
  motif. A lens, a leaf, a cup, a cursor, a ticket stub, a route on a map.
- **What does the brand already fix?** Logo colours, fonts, words it always uses, things it never
  does.
- **What is true and specific?** Real numbers, real names, real places and dates. They make a page
  credible, and invented ones mislead.
